Camera Degradation Health Score

A compact ADAS engineering study: controlled camera degradation → interpretable health signals → YOLOv8n downstream proxy → failure analysis → fallback decision.

Real road imageYOLOv8n proxyReproducible CILiterature-backed

1. Problem

A forward camera can become unreliable because of blur, glare/over-exposure, noise or contamination. The safety-relevant question is not only whether a detector changes, but whether the system has an independent indication that the input sensor itself is degraded.

This demo is a camera-health experiment, not a production functional-safety monitor.

Claim boundary

Our measurements: values in the benchmark table/plots below.

Paper evidence: findings cited in the Research section.

Heuristic: composite weights and the 0.65 threshold.

Not claimed: mAP, recall, missed-detection rate, statistical significance, or production safety validation.

2. Method

RGB road frame→ blur / glare / noise→ health metrics→ YOLOv8n proxy→ health state→ engineering fallback

Signals

Laplacian variance, edge density/retention, mean brightness, contrast, highlight saturation, shadow/highlight clipping and entropy. Detection count and mean YOLO confidence are deliberately kept as downstream proxy signals.

Composite health score

H = 0.40·sharpness_similarity + 0.15·edge_similarity + 0.10·brightness_similarity + 0.10·contrast_similarity + 0.25·clipping_quality

Similarity terms compare the corrupted frame with the clean experimental baseline. The score is deliberately interpretable; its weights are a lab heuristic, not learned safety calibration.

4. Failure case

Detector confidence is not a camera-health monitor. In this experiment, strong blur can collapse edge/sharpness information without necessarily producing a lower mean confidence for the boxes that remain. Strong glare reduces confidence, but even changes the raw detection count, which is not enough to tell whether those detections are correct.

There are two important failure mechanisms:

Glare: a large saturated region destroys local scene information while substantial edges remain elsewhere, so a blur-only metric can look acceptable. In the committed detector overlay, glare also produces a visibly implausible “airplane 0.45” box over the bus/glare region.

Impulse noise: artificial high-frequency edges can make Laplacian/edge values increase, so “more edges = healthier” is also false. The score therefore uses symmetric similarity to the clean baseline and a clipping penalty.

5. Engineering decision

H < 0.65 for 3 consecutive frames → DEGRADED

This is a demo heuristic. A production threshold requires fleet calibration, labeled health states, false-positive/false-negative analysis and safety validation.

Telemetry to log

Frame timestamp, exposure time, gain/ISO, shutter, auto-exposure state, brightness/clipping statistics and camera temperature when available.

Fallback discussion

Raise a degradation event, down-weight camera evidence in sensor fusion, and request a safer operating mode. Where the platform has independent Radar/LiDAR sensing, those modalities can support fallback. This repository does not implement vehicle sensor fusion or actuation.

Research connection

Yang et al., Sensors 2026. Interpretable lens-soiling severity plus temporal stabilization; reported Jitter (MAD) reduction of 51.5%. DOI

Limitations & reproducibility

This is a one-image controlled benchmark, not a fleet-scale validation. The corruption functions are synthetic; YOLO confidence/count lack ground-truth correctness; the health threshold is not safety-certified; spatially localized defects need ROI-aware analysis; real deployment needs temporal calibration across cameras and conditions.
