Camera Degradation Health Score

A compact ADAS engineering study: controlled camera degradation → interpretable health signals + upstream BREMOLA no-reference IQA → YOLOv8n downstream proxy → persistence policy → failure analysis → fallback decision.

1. Problem

A forward camera can become unreliable because of blur, glare/over-exposure, noise or contamination. The safety-relevant question is not only whether a detector changes, but whether the system has an independent indication that the input sensor itself is degraded.

This demo is a camera-health experiment, not a production functional-safety monitor.

Claim boundary

Our measurements: values in the benchmark table/plots below.

Paper evidence: findings cited in the Research section.

Heuristic: composite weights, the 0.65 threshold (set after the first run, then frozen) and the 3-frame persistence rule.

Hypothesis: statements labelled as such; not tested.

Not claimed: mAP, recall, missed-detection rate, statistical significance, or production safety validation.

2. Method

RGB road frame→ blur / glare / noise→ health metrics→ YOLOv8n proxy→ health state→ engineering fallback

Signals

Laplacian variance, edge density/retention, mean brightness, contrast, highlight saturation, shadow/highlight clipping and entropy. Plus the unmodified upstream BREMOLA script (Nam et al., Vehicles 2025; commit 7ba2699) as a no-reference blur score. Detection count, mean YOLO confidence and agreement with clean-image pseudo-labels are deliberately kept as downstream proxy signals; agreement is not recall.

Composite health score

H = 0.40·sharpness_similarity + 0.15·edge_similarity + 0.10·brightness_similarity + 0.10·contrast_similarity + 0.25·clipping_quality

Similarity terms compare the corrupted frame with the clean experimental baseline, so the score is reference-based and not deployable as-is. Weights are a lab heuristic set after the first 6-condition run and then frozen; the 4 milder glare/noise levels are out-of-sample.

4. Failure case

Selected case: glare. At gain 1.05, saturated pixels rise 1.27% → 20.00% and the detector loses the bus. Our health score is 0.636, just under a threshold tuned on this very frame. At gain 0.70 (11.42% saturated), the score says OK (0.803).

We observed: clipping has weight 0.25, so glare alone can lower H by at most 0.25. A global average under-weights a regional fault. Upstream BREMOLA rises slightly under glare (+3.8%).

Sources report: BREMOLA's authors place noise and glare outside the method's blur-only scope; our BREMOLA reading is consistent with that statement.

Hypothesis (not tested): for AEB/ACC tracking a bus as lead vehicle, a class flip at that moment could change the braking decision.

Also measured: mean confidence moves for reasons unrelated to image health. It is +3.0% under blur k = 21 (a wrong box replaced a weak one) and +13.1% under 0.5% noise (a weak box disappeared).

5. Engineering decision

H < 0.65 for 3 consecutive frames → DEGRADED

This is a demo heuristic. A production threshold requires fleet calibration, labeled health states, false-positive/false-negative analysis and safety validation.

Decision

(1) Do not use detector confidence as the health signal. (2) Add a glare-specific highlight-saturation rule beside the composite score. (3) Keep the 3-frame persistence rule. (4) Replace the clean reference with a rolling healthy-frame reference.

Verify next round

Flag rate per glare level on ≥20 images × 5 levels; the policy on real video with labelled fault intervals; real recall on a labelled nuScenes-C camera slice. Full criteria: REPORT §5.2.

Telemetry to log

Frame timestamp, exposure time, gain/ISO, shutter, auto-exposure state, brightness/clipping statistics and camera temperature when available.

Fallback discussion

Raise a degradation event, down-weight camera evidence in sensor fusion, and request a safer operating mode. Where the platform has independent Radar/LiDAR sensing, those modalities can support fallback. This repository does not implement vehicle sensor fusion or actuation.

Research connection

Dong et al., CVPR 2023. 27 corruption types and KITTI-C/nuScenes-C/Waymo-C; camera-only detectors are highly vulnerable to image corruptions. Paper
Xie et al., IEEE TPAMI 2025 (RoboBEV). Eight camera corruptions/failures at three severities and 33 BEV models; motivates testing more than clean accuracy. DOI
Nam et al., Vehicles 2025 (BREMOLA). No-reference blur quality using moving spectrum + Laplacian; Table 2 reports Laplacian SROCC 0.9236 among the compared HPFs. We run its code at commit 7ba2699. DOI · code
Yang et al., Sensors 2026. Interpretable lens-soiling severity plus temporal stabilization; reported Jitter (MAD) reduction of 51.5%. DOI

Limitations & reproducibility

This is a one-image controlled benchmark on a handheld street photo, not a vehicle-mounted camera frame or a fleet-scale validation. The corruptions are synthetic. The health score is reference-based and its weights were tuned on 5 of the 9 degraded conditions. YOLO outputs lack ground truth. The policy sequence is not real video. BREMOLA was run outside its tested regime (aspect stretch, Gaussian blur). The threshold is not safety-certified.
