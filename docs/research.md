## 1) Dong et al. — CVPR 2023 — 3D corruption benchmark

**Citation**

Y. Dong, C. Kang, J. Zhang, Z. Zhu, Y. Wang, X. Yang, H. Su, X. Wei, and J. Zhu, “Benchmarking Robustness of 3D Object Detection to Common Corruptions,” *Proceedings of the IEEE/CVF Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR)*, pp. 1022–1032, 2023.

- Paper: https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Dong_Benchmarking_Robustness_of_3D_Object_Detection_to_Common_Corruptions_CVPR_2023_paper.html
- Official code: https://github.com/thu-ml/3D_Corruptions_AD

### Problem
Clean KITTI/nuScenes/Waymo scores do not tell us how a detector behaves when weather, sensor noise, vehicle motion, unusual objects, or camera–LiDAR alignment corrupt the input.

### Input
Camera images and/or LiDAR point clouds from KITTI, nuScenes, and Waymo.

### Method
The authors design **27 common corruption types**, grouped into weather, sensor, motion, object, and alignment levels. Each has **five severity levels**. Camera-relevant corruptions include strong sunlight, Gaussian/uniform/impulse image noise, motion blur, and several corruptions shared across modalities.

### Output
Three corruption benchmarks: **KITTI-C, nuScenes-C, Waymo-C**, plus robustness results for 24 diverse 3D detectors.

### Datasets / metrics
- KITTI-C: AP and relative corruption error (RCE).
- nuScenes-C: mAP, NDS, corruption averages and RCE.
- Waymo-C: official Waymo detection metrics plus corruption aggregation.

### Main findings
- Motion-level corruptions were among the most damaging across models.
- LiDAR–camera fusion was generally more robust.
- Camera-only models were highly vulnerable to image corruptions.

### Important figures / tables
- **Figure 1** — taxonomy of the 27 corruptions across weather, sensor, motion, object, and alignment levels.
- **Table 1** — model families, modalities, representations, and detector heads used on KITTI-C and nuScenes-C.

### Assumptions / limitations
The benchmark applies controlled synthetic corruptions to existing datasets. This improves repeatability but does not reproduce every real physical camera fault or simultaneous multi-cause failure. It evaluates downstream perception robustness, **not an online camera-health monitor**.

### Relation to this demo
We follow the same experimental philosophy at much smaller scale: keep a clean reference scene, inject controlled corruption, then observe both input-health indicators and a downstream detector proxy.

## 2) Xie et al. — IEEE TPAMI 2025 — RoboBEV

**Citation**

S. Xie, L. Kong, W. Zhang, J. Ren, L. Pan, K. Chen, and Z. Liu, “Benchmarking and Improving Bird’s Eye View Perception Robustness in Autonomous Driving,” *IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence*, vol. 47, no. 5, pp. 3878–3894, 2025. DOI: **10.1109/TPAMI.2025.3535960**.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/TPAMI.2025.3535960
- Preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17426
- Official code: https://github.com/worldbench/RoboBEV

### Problem
High BEV performance on clean nuScenes does not establish robustness to lighting/weather corruption, motion blur, camera failure, frame loss, or multimodal sensor failure.

### Input
Multi-camera nuScenes imagery; camera + LiDAR for multimodal variants.

### Method
RoboBEV studies **eight camera-related corruption/failure types**, each at **three severity levels**:
- Brightness
- Darkness
- Fog
- Snow
- Motion Blur
- Color Quantization
- Camera Crash
- Frame Lost

The study evaluates **33 BEV models** over detection, map segmentation, depth estimation, and occupancy prediction, and also analyzes complete sensor-failure settings.

### Output
A corruption/failure benchmark and robustness analysis for BEV perception.

### Dataset / metrics
The benchmark is based on nuScenes-C. Detection evaluation uses nuScenes metrics such as NDS/mAP together with robustness-oriented summaries including mean Corruption Error (mCE) and mean Resilience Rate (mRR).

### Main findings
- Strong clean performance often helps absolute corrupted performance, but relative robustness can rank models differently.
- Pretraining and depth-free BEV transformations can improve robustness.
- Richer temporal information often improves robustness.
- A key qualitative observation from the RoboBEV analysis is that **motion blur can produce relatively small global pixel-distribution change while still causing a meaningful perception drop**.

### Important figures / tables
- **Figure 1** — benchmark design: perception tasks, sensor configurations, corruption/failure types, and severities.
- **Figure 2 (TPAMI/extended RoboBEV)** — pixel histograms under corruption; motion blur changes the global histogram relatively little while harming perception.
- Corruption-parameter/result tables define the corruption settings and compare robustness across models.

### Assumptions / limitations
The eight designed corruption classes cannot exhaust real OOD driving. RoboBEV evaluates model robustness, not whether a low-cost scalar health monitor can reliably diagnose the cause of image degradation.

### Relation to this demo
This motivates **multi-signal health monitoring**. Brightness/histogram statistics alone cannot reliably identify blur. Our demo therefore combines sharpness, edge, brightness/contrast and clipping signals, and keeps the downstream YOLO proxy separate.

## 3) Nam et al. — Vehicles 2025 — BREMOLA

**Citation**

W. Nam, T. Youn, and C. Ha, “No-Reference Image Quality Assessment with Moving Spectrum and Laplacian Filter for Autonomous Driving Environment,” *Vehicles*, vol. 7, no. 1, article 8, 2025. DOI: **10.3390/vehicles7010008**.

- Paper: https://doi.org/10.3390/vehicles7010008
- Code: https://github.com/woongchan789/BREMOLA

### Problem
A vehicle normally has no perfect reference image for each live camera frame. The work therefore develops a **no-reference image-quality metric** intended to quantify blur associated with camera deterioration/aging.

### Input
A single RGB driving image/frame. No pristine reference image is required by BREMOLA at inference time.

### Method
BREMOLA combines:
1. a Fourier “moving spectrum” term measuring high-frequency/sharpness behavior; and
2. a Laplacian-derived scene-complexity term.

The final metric is reported as:

`BREMOLA = A / sqrt(B)`

where **A** is a thresholded central-area statistic from the shifted/log Fourier spectrum and **B** is Laplacian-derived image complexity.

### Output
A scalar no-reference quality/blur score that can support a camera-degradation threshold.

### Dataset / metrics
The authors extract **45 real driving frames** from videos recorded in Dubai, Los Angeles and San Francisco and apply **20 blur levels** (average filters from 1×1 to 20×20), producing **900 samples**. They use Spearman rank correlation to study high-pass filters and analyze the metric under increasing blur.

### Main findings
- **Table 2:** Laplacian gives the largest reported SROCC (**0.9236**) among the tested high-pass filters; Canny is 0.8879 and Sobel 0.7113.
- The moving-spectrum statistic alone varies strongly with scene content; the Laplacian term is used to compensate for image complexity.
- The method distinguishes progressively blurred imagery more consistently after BREMOLA normalization.

### Important figures / tables / equation
- **Table 2** — high-pass-filter SROCC comparison; Laplacian = 0.9236.
- **Figure 12** — BREMOLA processing flow (FFT/log spectrum → area A; Laplacian complexity B → A/sqrt(B)).
- **Equation (8)** — `BREMOLA = A / sqrt(B)`.
- **Figure 17** — comparison before/after applying BREMOLA to the driving-frame blur experiment.

### Assumptions / limitations
The paper explicitly centers on **blur from aging/deterioration**. It notes that transient distortions such as **noise, glare, and motion artifacts are outside its main scope**.

### Relation to this demo
This is the closest literature match to our lightweight Laplacian-based health signal. However, our demo intentionally covers glare and impulse noise too, so we do **not** treat Laplacian variance as a universal health score. We add clipping, brightness, contrast and edge signals.

**We ran the upstream code.** `bremola.py` is a single CPU script (OpenCV + NumPy) with no bundled dataset ("For privacy reasons, we do not provide the dataset here"). We run it unmodified on every benchmark variant. Run conditions differ from the paper:
- the script resizes each frame to 1920×1080, which stretches our 810×1080 portrait input;
- we use Gaussian rather than average blur.

Measured result (REPORT §3, Observation E): BREMOLA drops 24.7% at the first blur level and then stays flat, and it rises slightly under glare and impulse noise. This is consistent with the paper's stated blur-only scope.

## 4) Yang et al. — Sensors 2026 — camera-lens soiling severity

**Citation**

F. Yang, X. Duan, F. Li, and L. Zhang, “A Static-to-Temporal Framework for Interpretable Camera Lens Soiling Severity Estimation in Autonomous Driving,” *Sensors*, vol. 26, no. 11, article 3533, 2026. DOI: **10.3390/s26113533**.

- Paper: https://doi.org/10.3390/s26113533

### Problem
Lens dirt/water/oil can reduce contrast, blur or occlude important image regions. Area-only or per-frame severity can also ignore opacity, spatial importance and temporal stability.

### Input
Camera images/video containing lens soiling.

### Method
The paper proposes:
- a **Structured Dual-Head Static Model (SDSM)** for tile-level four-class soiling distributions plus image-level severity;
- a structured severity score incorporating opacity, spatial importance and local dominance;
- synthetic temporal sequence generation (TS-SD);
- an adaptive EMA temporal module to stabilize severity over video.

### Output
An interpretable severity score plus temporally stabilized severity trajectories.

### Datasets / metrics
WoodScape, an External Test set, and OccNuScenes-Dirt. Reported metrics include rank correlation, downstream perception degradation, and temporal-stability measures.

### Main findings
- External Test cluster-level Spearman correlation is reported as **0.7876**.
- The temporal module reduces Jitter (MAD) from 0.0330 to 0.0160, a **51.5% reduction**, while largely preserving ranking.
- **Table 1:** downstream pedestrian detection rate drops as soiling severity rises (88.7% → 82.1% → 56.9% from Level 1 to Level 3 in the reported External Test).
- **Table 2:** OccNuScenes-Dirt shows strong BEV degradation with increasing dirt severity; multimodal variants reduce but do not eliminate the drop.

### Important figures / tables
- **Figure 1** — complete static-to-temporal framework.
- **Figure 5** — structured severity score and SDSM architecture.
- **Figure 8** — temporal trajectories before/after stabilization.
- **Table 1** — downstream pedestrian-detection degradation across severity levels.
- **Table 2** — BEV perception degradation across OccNuScenes-Dirt.
- **Table 8** — temporal stabilization results; Jitter (MAD) improves from 0.0330 to 0.0160.

### Assumptions / limitations
The method is specialized for lens soiling and uses learned models/data. It is far heavier than the Day 19 demo. Synthetic sequence generation is used to address scarcity of real temporal soiling data.

### Relation to this demo
Two engineering lessons transfer directly:
1. health severity should be **interpretable and multi-factor**, not merely contaminated area or one global statistic;
2. production monitoring needs **temporal persistence/stabilization**, which supports our “N consecutive frames” decision rule.

# Cross-paper synthesis

| Question | Literature answer | Demo decision |
|---|---|---|
| Do corruptions matter? | CVPR 2023 and RoboBEV show large robustness degradation under corruption/failure. | Inject controlled blur, glare and impulse noise. |
| Is one image statistic enough? | RoboBEV shows damaging corruptions may not strongly shift simple pixel distributions. | Combine sharpness, edge, brightness/contrast and clipping. |
| Is Laplacian defensible? | BREMOLA uses Laplacian to compensate image complexity; Table 2 reports SROCC 0.9236. | Keep Laplacian variance as one interpretable signal, not the whole monitor. |
| What about glare/soiling? | BREMOLA does not cover glare; soiling work explicitly models opacity/spatial effects. | Add highlight clipping and brightness deviation; document remaining spatial limitations. |
| Why N consecutive frames? | The 2026 soiling work shows temporal smoothing materially reduces severity jitter. | Use persistence before DEGRADED state; do not react to a single frame. |
| What should downstream do? | Multimodal robustness literature shows independent modalities can improve resilience. | Recommend logging, camera down-weighting and safer-mode/fusion fallback when supported; not implemented here. |

# What the literature does **not** justify

The papers do **not** validate our exact composite weights or threshold. The repository health score and threshold are a **demo heuristic calibrated only for a small controlled experiment**. Production use would require:
- representative fleet data;
- labeled camera-health states;
- per-camera/ISP calibration;
- spatial/ROI-aware health;
- temporal validation;
- downstream safety validation and false-alarm/miss analysis.
