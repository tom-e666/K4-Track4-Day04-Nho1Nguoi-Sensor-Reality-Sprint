# Day 19 Report — Camera Degradation Health Score for ADAS

**Topic:** T1 — Camera Degradation Health Score  
**Experiment:** one public road image, controlled corruption, interpretable health proxies, YOLOv8n downstream proxy  
**Reproducible evidence:** `results/metrics.csv`, `results/summary.json`, committed figures and detector overlays

## 1. Problem

An ADAS camera may temporarily or permanently become unreliable because of optical blur, glare/over-exposure, impulse noise, contamination or sensor/ISP faults. A detector can still produce confident boxes on a degraded image, so **detector confidence alone is not a reliable camera-health signal**.

This matters because corruption robustness work in autonomous driving has repeatedly shown that perception performance can degrade under image/sensor corruption. Dong et al. build KITTI-C, nuScenes-C and Waymo-C using 27 controlled corruptions and report especially high vulnerability for camera-only perception under image corruption [1]. RoboBEV further studies eight camera corruption/failure types at three severity levels across 33 BEV models [2].

Our engineering question is therefore:

> Can a lightweight, interpretable pre-perception monitor identify a degraded camera frame independently of downstream detector confidence?

---

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Pipeline

```text
RGB road image
→ controlled degradation
→ image-health metrics
→ composite health score
→ YOLOv8n downstream proxy
→ failure analysis
→ engineering fallback recommendation
```

### 2.2 Input and corruptions

The real-image benchmark downloads the public Ultralytics `bus.jpg` sample at runtime and records the URL + SHA-256 in `results/summary.json`.

Conditions:

1. Clean baseline
2. Gaussian blur, kernel 5
3. Gaussian blur, kernel 11
4. Gaussian blur, kernel 21
5. Strong local glare / over-exposure
6. Salt-and-pepper impulse noise

The design follows the *controlled corruption* philosophy used by corruption benchmarks such as Dong et al. [1] and RoboBEV [2], but our experiment is intentionally tiny and 2D.

### 2.3 Health metrics

We compute:

- **Laplacian variance** — sharpness/high-frequency proxy
- **edge density / edge retention** — Canny edge structure vs clean baseline
- **highlight saturation ratio** — grayscale pixels ≥245
- **shadow clipping ratio** — grayscale pixels ≤10
- **total clipped-pixel ratio**
- **mean brightness**
- **contrast (standard deviation)**
- **entropy**

The BREMOLA work of Nam et al. supports the use of Laplacian information for autonomous-driving image-quality assessment: its **Table 2** reports the highest tested SROCC for the Laplacian filter (**0.9236**) and its **Figure 12 / Equation (8)** combines a Fourier moving-spectrum term with Laplacian-derived image complexity [3]. However, that paper explicitly focuses on blur and does not claim to cover transient glare/noise, so our monitor must use more than Laplacian variance.

### 2.4 Composite demo health score

Each corrupted frame is compared with the clean experimental baseline.

```text
H =
  0.40 × sharpness_similarity
+ 0.15 × edge_similarity
+ 0.10 × brightness_similarity
+ 0.10 × contrast_similarity
+ 0.25 × clipping_quality
```

Sharpness and edge similarities are **symmetric ratios**: values that become much smaller *or much larger* than clean are penalized. This is important because impulse noise can artificially increase Laplacian/edge energy.

The weights were chosen as an **interpretable lab heuristic** to separate this controlled clean condition from the five stress variants. They are not trained on a labeled fleet dataset and may overfit this tiny experiment.

### 2.5 Downstream detector proxy

YOLOv8n is run on every variant. We record:

- detection count
- mean confidence of produced boxes

These values are **not detection correctness metrics**. There is no ground-truth annotation in this demo, so we do not report mAP/recall/miss rate.

### 3.3 Key observations

**Observation A — detector confidence does not track blur health.**  
At blur k=21, Laplacian variance falls from **3619.17 → 3.40** (about **99.91% lower**) and edge retention falls to **4.09%**. Yet YOLO mean confidence changes from **0.656 → 0.675**, approximately **+3.0%**. Detection count stays at 6.

That is the strongest result of this demo: a downstream model can remain confident on the subset of predictions it emits even though low-level image structure has collapsed.

**Observation B — glare is a different failure mode from blur.**  
Strong glare raises highlight-saturated pixels from **1.27% → 20.00%** (~15.7×) and total clipped pixels from **6.69% → 23.52%**. Mean YOLO confidence drops by **16.3%** (0.656 → 0.549), while raw detection count changes **6 → 7**. More detections do not imply better detection.

**Observation C — naive “sharpness = health” fails under impulse noise.**  
Salt-and-pepper noise makes Laplacian variance approximately **3.86× higher** than clean and edge retention reaches **139.2%**, even though the image is corrupted. The symmetric similarity design prevents this high-frequency artifact from being interpreted as “super healthy.”

## 4. Failure Case

### Primary failure: “confidence looks fine while the camera is visibly degraded”

Blur k=21 is the clearest case.

The input-health evidence is extreme:
- Laplacian: **−99.91%**
- edge retention: **4.09%**
- health score: **0.444 / DEGRADED**

But the detector proxy does not flag it:
- detections: **6 → 6**
- mean confidence: **+3.0%**

This happens because mean confidence is computed only over the boxes the detector chooses to output. It does not tell us that image information has globally collapsed, nor whether unreported objects were missed.

### Secondary failure: glare defeats blur-only monitoring

Under glare, Laplacian remains **2789.66**, roughly 77% of the clean value, and edge retention remains **87.3%** because large portions of the frame still contain edges. A blur-only rule could therefore classify the frame as acceptable even though **20% of grayscale pixels are near-white saturated**.

This supports a **multi-signal** monitor.

### Literature connection

RoboBEV reports that motion blur can cause meaningful perception degradation even when global pixel-distribution change is relatively small [2]. BREMOLA shows that scene complexity itself must be compensated when using frequency/edge information [3]. Yang et al. further show that severity estimation benefits from explicit multi-factor structure and temporal stabilization; their **Table 8** reports Jitter (MAD) reduction from 0.0330 to 0.0160 (**51.5%**) after adaptive EMA [4].

## 5. Engineering Decision

### Demo policy

```text
health_score < 0.65
for 3 consecutive frames
→ CAMERA_DEGRADED
```

Why persistence? A single frame may contain a transient reflection/noise spike. A temporal rule reduces chattering; this direction is also consistent with temporal-stability motivation in camera-soiling work [4].

**Important:** `0.65` and `3 frames` are demonstration choices only. A production threshold must be calibrated on representative fleet/video data with labeled health states and false-positive/false-negative costs.

### Telemetry to log on a real vehicle

- frame timestamp / sequence ID
- exposure time
- gain / ISO
- shutter
- auto-exposure state
- brightness / clipping health signals
- camera temperature when available
- camera/ISP diagnostic state
- health score + component scores over time

### Fallback recommendation

When persistent degradation is detected:

1. emit/log a camera-health degradation event;
2. down-weight camera evidence in fusion if the platform supports confidence-aware fusion;
3. request a safer operating mode / degrade affected ADAS functions;
4. rely more on independent Radar/LiDAR evidence where available.

This repository **does not implement sensor fusion, vehicle control, emergency braking, or a safety-certified state machine**.

## Limitations

- Only **one** public real road image is used in the committed benchmark.
- Corruptions are synthetically injected and do not cover all physical camera faults.
- No ground-truth labels → no mAP/recall/missed-detection claims.
- Composite weights and threshold are calibrated to a tiny controlled experiment.
- The score is frame-level and global; real systems need spatial/ROI-aware and sequence-level health.
- Different cameras, ISP pipelines, resolutions, weather and exposure settings will shift metric ranges.
