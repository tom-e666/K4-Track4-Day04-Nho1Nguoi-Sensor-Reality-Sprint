## 1. Problem

An ADAS camera may temporarily or permanently become unreliable because of optical blur, glare/over-exposure, impulse noise, contamination or sensor/ISP faults. A detector can still produce confident boxes on a degraded image, so **detector confidence alone is not a reliable camera-health signal**.

Sources report that perception degrades under image corruption: Dong et al. build KITTI-C, nuScenes-C and Waymo-C using 27 corruptions and report that camera-only detectors are highly vulnerable to image corruption [1]. RoboBEV studies eight camera corruption/failure types at three severity levels across 33 BEV models [2].

Engineering question:

> Can a lightweight, interpretable pre-perception monitor flag a degraded camera frame independently of downstream detector confidence — and where does it fail?

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Pipeline

```text
RGB frame → one controlled corruption (named parameter level)
  → image-health metrics → composite health score (reference-based)
  → upstream BREMOLA score (no-reference)
  → YOLOv8n proxy: count, mean confidence, agreement with clean pseudo-labels
  → persistence policy on a synthetic frame sequence
  → failure analysis → engineering decision
```

BREMOLA caveats: the upstream script resizes every frame to 1920×1080 (our 810×1080 portrait input is stretched about 2.4× horizontally), and the paper's blur uses **average filters**, whereas we use **Gaussian** blur. The repository's README states that the dataset is not provided.

### 2.3 Input and conditions (Step 3)

- Input: public Ultralytics `bus.jpg` (810×1080), SHA-256 `c02019c4…bfbc63`, downloaded at runtime; not committed.
- 1 baseline + 9 degraded conditions. Each changes **one** factor from the same baseline; the name encodes the parameter.

| Condition | Family | Parameters (full values in `summary.json → conditions`) |
|---|---|---|
| `clean` | baseline | none |
| `blur_gauss_k5` / `k11` / `k21` | Gaussian blur | kernel 5 / 11 / 21 px; σ = 1.1 / 2.0 / 3.5 px (OpenCV default for σ=0) |
| `glare_gain0.35` / `0.70` / `1.05` | local over-exposure | soft white disc, centre (591, 324) px, radius 243 px, falloff σ 81 px, additive gain 0.35 / 0.70 / 1.05 |
| `saltpepper_p0.005` / `0.010` / `0.025` | impulse noise | fraction of pixels flipped to 0 or 255 = 0.5% / 1.0% / 2.5%, RNG seed 19 |

All corruptions are deterministic (fixed seed). Rerunning gives byte-identical metrics on the same library versions.

### 2.4 Health metrics

Laplacian variance, Canny edge density and retention, highlight saturation (gray ≥245), shadow clipping (gray ≤10), total clipped ratio, mean brightness, contrast (std) and entropy. Directions and units are listed in §0.

### 2.5 Composite demo health score

```text
H = 0.40·sharpness_similarity + 0.15·edge_similarity + 0.10·brightness_similarity
  + 0.10·contrast_similarity + 0.25·clipping_quality          DEGRADED if H < 0.65
```

- Sharpness and edge terms are **symmetric ratios** to the clean frame, so values far above clean (noise) are penalized as well as values far below (blur).
- **H is reference-based:** it needs a clean image of the same scene, which a moving vehicle never has. It is a lab instrument, not a deployable monitor (see Limitations and §5).
- **Chronology, stated honestly.** The weights and the 0.65 threshold were set in commit `40af73b` *after* seeing the first 6-condition run, so they are fitted to `blur k5/11/21`, `glare_gain1.05` and `saltpepper_p0.025`. They were then **frozen**. The four new levels (`glare_gain0.35/0.70`, `saltpepper_p0.005/0.010`) are an out-of-sample test of that frozen rule.

### 2.6 Downstream detector proxy

YOLOv8n (`ultralytics==8.4.172`) runs on every variant. We record:

- detection count and mean confidence;
- **agreement with clean pseudo-labels**: the fraction of clean-image boxes re-found with the same class at IoU ≥ 0.5, plus the lists of lost and new boxes.

Agreement is **not recall**. The clean-image detections are YOLO's own output, so their mistakes are inherited as "truth".

### 3.3 Observations (all measured)

**A — Mean confidence rises under blur, and the rise comes from a wrong box.**  
At `blur_gauss_k21`, Laplacian variance falls 3619.17 → 3.40 (−99.91%) and edge retention falls to 4.1%, yet mean confidence goes 0.656 → 0.675 (+3.0%). The detection count stays 6 → 6, but the box set changes: the clean `stop sign 0.26` is lost and a new `dog 0.36` appears (agreement 83%). The new wrong box scores higher than the box it replaced, which lifts the average. An unchanged count therefore does **not** mean unchanged output.

**B — Mean confidence also rises when a weak box disappears.**  
At `saltpepper_p0.005`, the only change in the detector output is that `stop sign 0.26` disappears (count 6 → 5), and mean confidence jumps +13.1% (0.656 → 0.741). At `saltpepper_p0.025`, `bus` confidence drops 0.87 → 0.57 and a `dog 0.30` appears. Mean confidence is an aggregate that moves in either direction for reasons unrelated to image health.

**C — Strong glare replaces the bus detection.**  
At `glare_gain1.05`, highlight saturation rises 1.27% → 20.00% (~15.7×) and mean confidence drops −16.3%. The `bus 0.87` box is lost and replaced by `airplane 0.45` and `truck 0.29` over the glare region, so count *increases* 6 → 7 while the largest object is mis-classified (`results/detector_before_after.png`).

**D — The frozen health rule misses moderate glare and light noise.**  
`glare_gain0.70` has 11.42% saturated pixels but H = 0.803 → **OK**. `saltpepper_p0.005/0.010` score H = 0.832 / 0.749 → **OK**, although the detector already lost a box at both levels. Only the levels the weights were tuned on cross 0.65, and only by 0.014–0.021. On the synthetic smoke scene, even `glare_gain1.05` scores 0.680 → OK.

**E — Upstream BREMOLA detects blur onset but not severity, and reads glare/noise as "sharper".**  
BREMOLA drops 38.91 → 29.30 (−24.7%) at k5, then stays flat (29.58 at k11, 28.56 at k21). It *rises* under glare (+3.8% at gain 1.05) and noise (+5.5% to +6.4%).

**F — The persistence rule removes transient alarms at a 2-frame cost.**  
On a 23-frame synthetic sequence (1-frame glare flash, 2-frame noise burst, then persistent `blur_gauss_k21`), single-frame thresholding raises 3 alarms on transient frames, while the 3-consecutive-frame policy raises **0**. The policy alarms 2 frames after blur onset (frame 17 vs onset 15), about 67 ms at an assumed 30 fps (`results/policy_timeline.png`, `results/policy_sequence.csv`).

## 4. Failure case (Step 5)

### Selected case: `glare_gain1.05` and the glare sweep

**Sensor fault and level.** Local over-exposure: additive white disc with gain 1.05, radius 243 px, centred on the upper-right of the bus.

**We observed (measured):**
- highlight saturation 1.27% → 20.00%, clipped pixels 6.69% → 23.52%, entropy 7.925 → 7.268 bits;
- the detector loses `bus 0.87` and outputs `airplane 0.45` + `truck 0.29` (agreement 83%, count 6 → 7, mean confidence −16.3%);
- the health score reaches 0.636, only 0.014 below a threshold that was tuned on this very frame;
- at `glare_gain0.70` (11.42% saturated pixels) the health score is 0.803 → OK, so the composite would not flag it;
- BREMOLA rises (+3.8%), i.e. a blur-only no-reference metric would rate the glare frame slightly *better*.

**Sources report:** BREMOLA's authors scope the method to blur from camera deterioration and state that noise, glare and motion artifacts are temporary distortions outside that scope [3]. Our measurement is consistent with that scope statement; it is not a contradiction of their results. RoboBEV reports that corruptions with small global pixel-distribution change (motion blur) can still harm perception [2]. Yang et al. report that per-frame severity jitters and that adaptive EMA reduces Jitter (MAD) 0.0330 → 0.0160 [4]. These numbers come from other datasets and metrics and are not comparable to ours.

**Impact on the feature:** in this image, the detector's view of the largest object changes class under strong glare (measured). *(Hypothesis)* For an AEB/ACC function that tracks a bus as a lead vehicle, a class flip or lost track at this moment could change the braking decision. We did not run a tracker or a planner.

**Why the monitor misses moderate glare** (arithmetic of the formula, plus a hypothesis): the clipping term carries weight 0.25, so even a total loss of that term lowers H by at most 0.25. If the other four terms stayed at 1.0, H could not go below 0.75. Glare is flagged only when it *also* reduces global sharpness, edge, brightness or contrast similarity. At `glare_gain0.70` those four terms still contribute 0.659 of their 0.75 maximum. *(Hypothesis)* Any regional fault that leaves most of the frame intact will be under-weighted by a global average in the same way.

**Limitations of this analysis:** one image; the glare is synthetic and additive, with no lens flare or bloom; there is no ground truth, so we cannot say whether the detector's output at `glare_gain0.70` is correct, only that it matches the clean output.

## 5. Engineering decision

### 5.1 Decision

1. **Do not use detector confidence or detection count as the camera-health signal** (Observations A–C).
2. **Add a glare-specific rule beside the composite score.** Flag the frame when highlight-saturation ratio exceeds the per-camera clean baseline by a calibrated margin, independent of H. A single global average hides regional glare (§4).
3. **Keep the persistence rule** (`H < 0.65` or glare flag, for 3 consecutive frames → `CAMERA_DEGRADED`). Measured on the synthetic sequence: 3 → 0 transient alarms, 2-frame latency.
4. **Replace the clean reference before any deployment.** Candidates: a rolling reference from recent frames already judged healthy, or a no-reference metric. BREMOLA covers blur onset only (Observation E), so it would need glare/noise companions.

On `CAMERA_DEGRADED`:

- log a health event;
- down-weight camera evidence in fusion if the platform supports confidence-aware fusion;
- request a safer operating mode or degrade the affected ADAS functions;
- rely more on independent radar/LiDAR evidence where available.

**This repository does not implement fusion, vehicle control or a safety-certified state machine.**

### 5.2 How to verify the improvement next round

| Change | Metric / log | Pass criterion (proposed) |
|---|---|---|
| Glare-specific saturation rule | Flag rate per glare level on ≥20 images × 5 gain levels; agreement with clean pseudo-labels per level | Every level at which mean agreement drops below 100% is flagged on ≥90% of images; clean false-flag rate ≤5% |
| Rolling reference instead of the clean image | Same table recomputed with the reference built from the previous N healthy frames of a real video clip | Ranking of conditions by H unchanged (Spearman ρ ≥0.9 vs the clean-reference H) |
| Persistence policy | `policy_sequence.csv` on a **real** video with labelled glare/blur intervals | 0 alarms on intervals shorter than 3 frames; alarm latency ≤3 frames on persistent faults |
| Detector-side check | Labelled subset (e.g. a nuScenes-C camera slice) | Report real recall per condition so the proxy can be validated or rejected |

### 5.3 Telemetry to log on a real vehicle

Frame timestamp and sequence ID; exposure time, gain/ISO and auto-exposure state; per-frame health components (sharpness, edges, clipping, brightness, contrast); health score and policy state; camera temperature and ISP diagnostics when available.

## Limitations

- **One** public image (`bus.jpg`): a handheld street photo, not a vehicle-mounted ADAS camera frame (different optics, mounting, ISP and resolution).
- Corruptions are synthetic. Glare is an additive disc with no flare, bloom or exposure-control response; noise is i.i.d. salt-and-pepper.
- The health score is **reference-based** (needs the clean frame of the same scene) and therefore not deployable as-is.
- Weights and threshold were tuned after the first run on 5 of the 9 degraded conditions; only 4 conditions are out-of-sample.
- No ground truth. Agreement with clean pseudo-labels inherits YOLO's clean-image mistakes; no mAP/recall claims.
- The policy sequence reuses single-image variants; it is not real video, so temporal correlation and gradual onset are not modelled.
- BREMOLA was run outside its tested regime (aspect-ratio stretch to 1920×1080, Gaussian instead of average blur, glare/noise out of scope).
- Results on other cameras, ISPs, resolutions, weather and exposure settings will shift metric ranges.
